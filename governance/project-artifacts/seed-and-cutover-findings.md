## 8. Research — what current practice says and where it changed the scripts

| Topic | Finding | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Idempotent seeds** — `INSERT … ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` needs a unique constraint but takes no lock on the conflicting row and is the cheapest idempotent insert; `MERGE` (PG 15+) needs no constraint but behaves differently under concurrency ([pganalyze](https://pganalyze.com/blog/5mins-postgres-15-merge-vs-insert-on-conflict), [Chat2DB](https://chat2db.ai/resources/blog/postgres-merge-vs-on-conflict), [Seedfast](https://seedfa.st/blog/postgres-seed-script)) | Every keyed table uses `ON CONFLICT` on its real unique constraint; `FIN_RULE_LINE` (no key) uses a `NOT EXISTS` on "rule has no lines". Changed: I had intended `MERGE` for the chart — dropped it, the constraint exists and `ON CONFLICT` is the documented idiom in this repo (V34's loop is the precedent) |
| **Fiscal calendars** — 12 monthly periods is standard; ERPs add a period-0/opening and a period-13/adjustment period so opening and closing entries do not pollute a trading month ([Oracle EPM](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/enterprise-performance-management-common/erpia/fus_gl_adjustment_periods_100xee85855b.html), [TechnoFunc](https://www.technofunc.com/index.php/functional-skills2/general-ledger/item/adjustment-period), [Sage 300](https://help.sage300.com/en-us/2026/classic/Content/Financials/General_Ledger/Setup/AboutFiscalYearsAndPeriods.htm)) | **Not adopted**: FIN v1 has no period type, `generatedPeriod` produces exactly 12 calendar months, and `API-FIN-027` posts the opening entry into "the next year's first period by `periodNo`". A 13th period would break that path. Seed = 12 months, January carries the opening entry. Recorded as feedback for the factory, not as a seed deviation |
| **Chart hierarchy** — adjacency list (parent id) is the recommended default; closure table / `ltree` only when ancestor queries dominate ([Ackee](https://www.ackee.agency/blog/hierarchical-models-in-postgresql), [AppMaster](https://appmaster.io/blog/model-org-charts-postgresql-adjacency-vs-closure), [dev.to](https://dev.to/dowerdev/implementing-hierarchical-data-structures-in-postgresql-ltree-vs-adjacency-list-vs-closure-table-2jpb)) | FIN v1 is an adjacency list (`parent_account_id`) — matches practice; the seed keeps Oracle's prefix codes so a path is recoverable from the code alone, and inserts level-by-level so the FK is satisfied without a second pass |
